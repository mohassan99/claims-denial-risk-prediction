# DMEPOS prior authorization process overview

## Purpose

The prior authorization program for durable medical equipment, prosthetics, orthotics and supplies (DMEPOS) helps protect the Medicare Trust Fund from improper payments while making sure beneficiaries can get the items they need in a timely way. Prior authorization checks that applicable Medicare coverage, payment and coding requirements are met before the item is delivered.

## How items are selected

CMS keeps a Master List of DMEPOS items that may require prior authorization or a face-to-face encounter with a written order before delivery. The list is updated at least once a year based on items with patterns of unnecessary utilization. Items on the Required Prior Authorization List are chosen by evaluating DMEPOS categories for problematic billing trends. Recent additions include orthoses, power mobility devices, pressure reducing support surfaces, lower limb prosthetics and pneumatic compression devices. Selected items usually start in some geographic regions and then expand nationwide.

## Claims without prior authorization

For items on the Required Prior Authorization List, prior authorization is a condition of payment. A claim submitted without an affirmative prior authorization decision, or without a provisional affirmation that meets the 90% compliance threshold, is not eligible for payment.

Standard requests are decided within 7 calendar days (effective January 1, 2025). Expedited requests are decided within 2 business days. Suppliers furnishing items under urgent circumstances can use specific modifiers, such as ST, to indicate an emergency, which may bypass the standard timelines.

## Submitting requests

Suppliers can submit prior authorization requests before delivering the item. A request needs the clinical documentation that supports medical necessity under Medicare coverage rules. A supplier with a provisional affirmation rate of 90% or higher may qualify for an exemption from prior authorization, established in December 2025, with the first exemption cycle starting June 1, 2026 and repeating annually. For power mobility device bases, suppliers may voluntarily request authorization for qualifying accessories using a separate voluntary list. Suppliers should contact their DME MAC or its website for jurisdiction-specific submission procedures, timeframes and documentation requirements.
