# Coverage indications for PAP devices in obstructive sleep apnea

## Initial Medicare coverage requirements

Three foundational criteria must be met. The item must fall within a defined Medicare benefit category. It must be reasonable and necessary for diagnosing or treating illness or injury or improving the function of a malformed body member. All other applicable statutory and regulatory requirements must be met.

The policy distinguishes E0601 (single-level continuous positive airway pressure, CPAP) from E0470 (bi-level respiratory assist device without backup rate). Both treat obstructive sleep apnea when specific conditions are documented.

## E0601 coverage criteria

Three conditions must all be met. First, the beneficiary has an in-person clinical evaluation by the treating practitioner before sleep testing, to assess for obstructive sleep apnea. Second, the beneficiary completes a sleep test that meets one of two thresholds: an apnea-hypopnea index (AHI) or respiratory disturbance index (RDI) of at least 15 events per hour with a minimum of 30 events, or an AHI or RDI of 5 to 14 events per hour with a minimum of 10 events together with documented excessive daytime sleepiness, impaired cognition, mood disorders, insomnia, hypertension, ischemic heart disease, or history of stroke. Third, the beneficiary is given instruction by the supplier in the proper use and care of the device.

## E0470 requirements

A beneficiary qualifies for E0470 only after meeting all E0601 criteria plus an additional one: an E0601 has been tried and proven ineffective based on a therapeutic trial. Ineffective means documented failure to meet therapeutic goals despite optimal therapy, including proper mask selection and appropriate pressure adjustment. If E0601 proves ineffective during the initial facility-based titration or home trial, moving to E0470 needs no new clinical evaluation or sleep test.

## Continued coverage beyond three months

Between the 31st and 91st day after therapy starts, the treating practitioner must conduct a clinical re-evaluation and document that the beneficiary is benefiting from PAP therapy. Two things are needed: an in-person re-evaluation documenting improved obstructive sleep apnea symptoms, and objective evidence of adherence available for the practitioner to review. Adherence means use of PAP for at least 4 hours per night on 70% of nights during a consecutive 30-day period at any time in the first 3 months of use.

## Replacement and transitions

If a device must be replaced during its 5-year reasonable useful lifetime because of loss, theft or irreparable damage, no new evaluation, sleep test or trial is required. After the 5-year lifetime, replacement needs only an in-person evaluation documenting continued use and benefit, with no new sleep test. Moving from E0601 to E0470 after more than 3 months of use needs a new clinical re-evaluation but no sleep test, and a new 3-month trial starts with the E0470.

## Failed initial trial

A beneficiary who fails the initial 12-week trial can requalify with two things: an in-person clinical re-evaluation to determine why treatment failed, and a repeat facility-based sleep study.
